## Verdict

**Publish after the listed changes; hold the current v1.19 package.** The reference values, seven sector assignments, two sets of seven locked configs, immutable prediction bytes, registration prefixes and supplied synthetic tests check out. The author has made the previously missing product-choice decision. However, its implementation can turn ordinary metadata-fetch failures into a definitive QLP designation, does not establish the claimed completeness of the lists, and accepts fallback or acquisition histories inconsistent with the declared sequence. These are repairable procedure and validation defects. They do not require changing any frozen prediction or replacing S111 as the primary. The scientific evaluator and dilution caveat can remain; repair and re-check the designation/state controls before publication. Evidence and exact corrections follow.

### Sector end, checkpoint and exact declaration

The N+1 safeguard is useful: `target_list_designation.py:93–105` will not designate from a table that has no next-sector row, and the supplied artificial tests exercise that refusal. But presence of an N+1 row is an operational completeness heuristic, not proof that the schedule is final. Declare that the saved official table supplies the operative end, how later schedule corrections are handled, and how uncertainty delays designation. Freeze the accepted end with the first decision receipt; do not recalculate cutoffs opportunistically from a later table. This assessment is based on the code, not a claim that the actual official table is wrong.

The prospective, weekly, retained-record wording is materially better than a discretionary “first scoring attempt” (`make_configs_s111.py:41–47`). Nevertheless, “first check on or after end” with weekly monitoring is different from “on each sector's end date” in DECISIONS 178(a). A short, dated author-approved clarification should define the first scheduled check/window and missed-check disposition, rather than silently describing it as exactly unchanged. Periodic monitoring cannot establish continuous availability. A final check within 24 hours before a cutoff does not establish absence throughout the remaining hours; the declared recorded-selection policy must say that expressly. These are part of S111-2–4, not additional independent blockers.

Suggested replacement governance wording, to be completed with the author's chosen scheduling window:

> The product decision uses the first successful, complete metadata snapshot in the declared post-sector-end checking window. Failed or incomplete queries remain PENDING and are retained. The accepted snapshot and sector-end timestamp are fixed before acquisition of either product's flux. Later metadata cannot replace that decision. Timely selection and fallback are determined from the retained checking history, not from a single later inventory. A missed or unresolved deadline is reported explicitly and is not treated as proof of non-availability. The +365-day final disposition cannot be reopened by a later first acquisition. This is periodic recorded-check evidence, not a claim of continuously observing the archive.

The final text must additionally state the chosen target-version and DDT scope and whether “recorded selection” replaces “delivery.” It must be reflected consistently in config strings, appended registration correction, decision log and validator, with regenerated code/config locks. Preserve previous text and all frozen prediction bytes.

- **Additional adversarial runs:** `[reviewer temporary path redacted]` mocks `fetch()` and time, then calls the production snapshot derivation and validator; `[reviewer temporary path redacted]` uses only the freshly generated artificial light curves and internally consistent synthetic records. Exact passing failures and dates are reported above so conclusions do not depend on retaining temporary files. No genuine archive records were represented as newly fetched evidence.
- **Saved S108 metadata demo:** independently re-derived both designations exactly, checked saved-byte hashes, and reproduced end `2026-10-04T20:30:00Z`, +120 d `2027-02-01T20:30:00Z`, +365 d `2027-10-04T20:30:00Z`. The saved manifest contains 101 HTTP-200 records and one HTTP-404 record; TIC317134140 is in its sector target list, TIC24526743 is absent from its saved lists/CTLs. Verification: production `derive()` and `[reviewer temporary path redacted]`. This authenticates internal consistency of the saved demo, not live server completeness or future S111 list availability.
- **TLS handling:** source `target_list_designation.py:29–38` invokes system `curl` without `-k`, records HTTP status, and documents the Python/certifi problem. Using a normally verifying system client is acceptable. I did not reproduce the network certificate failure or independently certify the client trust store. The blocker is ignoring recorded HTTP failures, not use of curl.
- **QLP split validation:** source inspection of `validate_round4_inputs.py` and the fresh QLP suite supports its separate config/prior/clock/output binding. Splitting route validation from clock/scoring validation is conceptually coherent, provided a retained final receipt requires both for the same config and fixed designation. Existing byte checks do not establish when the product choice was made; S111-4 addresses that distinction.
- **Not verified:** no raw training photometry, real test photometry, new clock calculation, real delivery/acquisition, statistical power or host attribution was examined. I did not independently establish that every historical target-list version remains available, or that an arbitrary local record could not have been manually falsified. The concrete failures require neither assumption. I did not treat source-file timestamps or a mutable decision log as external proof of past non-examination.

The minimum next step is a focused correction of S111-1–4, the isolated refusal test, and the corresponding dated wording/locks, followed by a synthetic re-check. This report is not permission to access test photometry or to publish.
