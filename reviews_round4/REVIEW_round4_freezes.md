### 188664994: exclusion, depth and predictive uncertainty

**F8 / moderate: formal timing errors need a more explicit interpretation.** The reported χ² = 201.0630 for 15 degrees of freedom and inflation √(χ²/15) = 3.661175 reproduce. The quoted maximum 15.4-min uncertainty is the uncertainty of a fitted *linear mean ephemeris*, conditional on multiplying all sector errors by the same factor. It is not a calibrated 68% prediction interval for a new sector with an additional unknown timing systematic. The frozen text, line 9, flags scatter, which is useful, but does not make this distinction. Independent fits to the saved `sector_times` give residual RMS 22.93 min; leaving out S84 shifts the epoch-753 forecast by −21.74 min, while leaving out S81 shifts it by +15.03 min. An illustrative constant extra-jitter model yielding χ²/dof = 1 requires 21.73 min of jitter and gives 20.26 min uncertainty on the epoch-753 mean, or 29.70 min for a new sector including that jitter. These are sensitivity results, **not a uniquely correct replacement uncertainty**. Verification: `/tmp/round4_review/timing_sensitivity.py` and `.json`, using only `final_ephemeris_v2.json`.

Required wording: “The listed σ values are formal uncertainties of the linear ephemeris after a common error-inflation factor. They do not include an independently calibrated additional sector timing systematic. Sector residuals have RMS 22.9 min, and leaving one sector out changes the final listed prediction by up to 21.7 min. This is an exploratory, timing-scattered ephemeris test; its nominal 3σ gate has not been shown to have 99.7% predictive coverage.” Retaining the declared ephemeris is acceptable if this limitation is explicit. If an alternative noise model is adopted, it must be a new dated version fixed before test data, with both versions retained; do not enlarge the gate after seeing the test outcome.

The S26 exclusion itself is justified by the existing data quality, even though it was chosen after an initial dry run. With QUALITY = 0 and finite flux, normalizing by each light curve's median, taking 6-h median bins anchored on its first cadence, the p99−p1 ranges are 111.834% for SPOC PDCSAP and 14.660% for QLP SAP. The point-to-point estimator `1.4826 median(|diff(f/median(f))|)/sqrt(2)` gives 9030.42 ppm and 1572.30 ppm respectively. The S17/S20/S23/S24 control ranges reproduce the quoted approximately 1840–2050 ppm (SPOC) and 597–683 ppm (QLP). Verification: permitted S17–26 FITS files through `/tmp/round4_review/quality.py` and `quality.log`. The range statements are correct under this estimator; state that estimator if these numbers are published.

Including S26 reproduces its fitted depth 12378.51 ppm, timing offset +12.0 ±33 min, and O−C +12.38 min. The all-sector fit then has χ²/dof approximately 201.2/16 and inflation 3.55, rather than 201.1/15 and 3.66 without S26. The future forecast changes only by seconds. Thus it is not a timing outlier whose removal artificially tightened the ephemeris. Verification: a separate permitted dry run without `--exclude-sectors`, `/tmp/round4_review/including_S26.log`. This supports a defensible data-quality exclusion; it does not establish that the decision was made without seeing any in-sample outcomes. The log should state the actual order openly.

## Script changes and preservation

The changed horizon constant is correct for extending the numerical Julian-date horizon to 2028-01-01 rather than 2027-01-01, and it is changed in both the normal and undated-fallback loops (`scripts/prereg_v2.py:123,133`). The day-boundary comparison is applied to BJD_TDB, so the comment's “00:00 UTC” is not an exact geocentric-UTC cutoff; this minute-scale distinction does not explain the year-long omissions found here. The output includes a separate `utc_tdb_scale_only` column; the `utc` column uses the target light-travel correction (`prereg_v2.py:12–17,127`). I have not revalidated every future UTC minute against a second solar-system ephemeris in this focused review.

`--exclude-sectors` requires a non-empty reason; excluded QLP sectors are skipped before reading, excluded SPOC sectors before timing/refitting but after loading (`prereg_v2.py:61–85`). That is suitable for an in-sample quality exclusion; it must not be mistaken for the separate holdout guard. The actual holdout filter excludes declared future sectors and every sector ≥106 before QLP reading or SPOC download/loading (`prereg_v2.py:58–83`). The exclusion and reason are saved in JSON and text (`prereg_v2.py:141,153`). The removed TESScut fallback in the generic outcome template is appropriate, subject to F4's distinction between flux extraction and clock metadata.

The optional explicit ephemeris path in `qlp_clock_v2.py:51–57` works for these new-object dry-run inputs and records the path in the clock JSON. It does not validate that an arbitrary supplied JSON belongs to the requested TIC; adding that assertion is sensible defensive hardening, but the two actual inputs and results checked here are the correct ones. The 89247735 special-case override still takes precedence over the optional path; document that exception if advertising the argument as general-purpose.

The pinned evaluator hash was also checked against the actual published bundle path: both `scripts/prereg_conformance.py` and `public_release/conformance_v2.1/scripts/prereg_conformance.py` have SHA-256 `a864b07ace8d3220a2d94a60513a488c8a5d87b54f41ebf510ff40ef1755311f` (`shasum -a 256`). The published file lives inside the conformance bundle, rather than at `public_release/scripts/prereg_conformance.py`.

The new CLI guard in `ffi_pixel_test.py:89–91` executes before catalog loading, target lookup or any cutout access. It rejects the seven protected S106 TICs wholesale and any requested sector ≥106. Thus it is stricter than the sector-only prohibition for those seven, and it also covers the new targets' forbidden sectors. This is a command-line guard, not a guarantee against bypassing the CLI by importing and directly calling internal functions. Direct refusal tests also passed: with `MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/round4_review/mpl`, `.venv/bin/python -B -I scripts/ffi_pixel_test.py 151767028 2.2104 4056.1 1 --sectors 106` exited 1 with the guard message; a protected-TIC test for 115692561 with `--sectors 96` likewise refused before data access.

Earlier frozen results were not rewritten: after the reproductions, all 42 source CSV hashes still match their frozen JSON/Markdown records, and `git -C public_release status --short` remains empty. These checks establish preservation of the inspected frozen products and the public working tree, not a universal byte audit of every unrelated local file.

## Publication gate and limits

The minimum publication gate is: correct 343298093's credit and the faulty prior-search results (F1/F5); publish the complete horizon erratum with versioned handling of every affected record (F3); lock the three objects' scoring configs, missing c0 prior, first-sector and refusal rules, and precise TESScut clock exception (F2/F4); append the 188664994 uncertainty/provenance clarifications (F8/F9); and correct the smaller credit, scope and STATUS errors (F6/F7). A new candidate being weak or having elevated FPP is not itself a reason to suppress a clearly labelled pre-data ephemeris test. Conversely, a reproduced hash is not enough to make an incomplete scoring contract or incorrect priority claim publication-ready.

This review does not approve publishing v1.15 as it stands, validate any planet, assign any transit host, or certify forecast-uncertainty coverage. No protected S106+ or S111+ test photometry was accessed. The pixel, TRICERATOPS and complete vetting pipelines were not rerun; their saved classifications and numerical outputs were checked as described. Public provenance of the compressed eumemic table was checked live at the cited immutable commit. ExoFOP checks used the dated local snapshots, and the RAVEN association was checked in two supplied catalog sources rather than its original primary table. The dates on local freezes and decision logs establish a recorded chronology; proving when a person first saw data or when an unpublished local decision was made is outside what these files can demonstrate.
