# Independent review of conformance-v2.1-rc1

Date: 2026-10-20. Reviewer/tool: OpenAI Codex. Working directory: `/Users/fernando/Desktop/NASA`.

Reviewed evaluator SHA-256: `590502679b335e8b93f7f30612dda58894112fbbf83f0fb62f8b0acbaf5ac684`.

Sources actually opened: the requested Python scripts, seven configurations and registrations, prediction CSVs and ephemeris JSONs, protocol TeX, clarification and governance drafts, decision log, internal findings JSON, the design/forecast outputs in `results/conformance_v21/`, and relevant prior-sector files and review outputs. I also inspected `scripts/conformance_calib_report.py`, which is material to the proposed statistical claims but absent from the procedure's code table. Tests used synthetic data and permitted S69/S96/S105 data; the forecast read earlier-sector timing/quality patterns and design-sector noise. The S105 input test called the JPL Horizons vector API through the clock tool. I did not inspect any S106-or-later target photometry or cutout, including its FITS headers, and did not invoke `--declared-test`. This review does not independently establish public-release chronology on GitHub/Zenodo or current S106 archive availability.

## Verdict

**Publish after listed fixes. Do not publish this release candidate as the operative scoring procedure yet.** The core repairs are substantial: the input/clock gates work in the supplied tests, EXCLUDED now searches the timing gate, the split is genuinely part of the reported outcome, and many departures are candidly identified. The completed holdout products arrived during this review and their 1,080 results check against the final evaluator SHA. However, the proposed effective-N and family-wise confidence calculations are not statistically valid as labelled; the edge cuts do not satisfy their stated numerical selection criterion; and the original 40-run acceptance rule for the split failed, rather than being retrospectively satisfied by expanding to 160 runs. There is also a reproducible missing-event/NaN issue in EXCLUDED, an unenforced object-specific override, and unfinished precedence/release documentation. These are repairable before target photometry is examined. Publication should describe a newly amended operational test, not imply literal restoration of all original protocol clauses or validated seven-target error control. Evidence for this judgment is itemised below; “disclosed” in the historical table is not equivalent to “scientifically validated.”

### Edge-rule and decision-order assessment

The implemented edge inequalities are internally clear. `edge_trim` removes `[start,start+1.5 d)` after the sector start or a gap strictly greater than 0.5 d, and `(end-0.5 d,end]` before the sector end or a gap strictly greater than 0.1 d. Equality at 1.5/0.5 d is retained; the start/end cadence is removed. Gaps are computed once on finite, QUALITY=0 data, before the clock shift; newly trimmed gaps are not iterated. I found no general one-cadence indexing error in this function. This assessment comes from the function and the independent forecast rerun, not from test-sector data.

The CSV recomputation reproduces the quoted post-resume fractions (0.3081, 0.2500, 0.0705, 0.0894, 0.0861, 0.0579) and first two pre-long-gap fractions (0.1193, 0.1657). The discrepancy is the inference that **every** later bin meets the threshold, not those quoted fractions. Unrestricted application to all populated bins in the long-gap profile would push the cut to approximately 12 days because distant bins can approach the opposite edge and have small occupancy. That is a diagnostic of an incompletely specified criterion, not a sensible recommended cut. Evidence: `audit_tables.py` and its grouped counts.

Keeping a single pooled rule can be scientifically defensible: it uses larger samples and avoids selecting target-by-target cuts to rescue preferred tests. Log 80 itself shows option B changes which tests retain coverage, and log 82 retains option A. I find no basis to infer test-outcome-driven tuning, because I have not seen evidence of target S106 flux examination. Nevertheless, pooled calibration does not guarantee equal per-target error rates. The public document must expose the target-specific failures rather than treating pooling as proof of individual calibration. Local log entries describe declarations before design runs; their day-level timestamps and filesystem mtimes do not independently prove that ordering. I verified consistency with stored results, not an immutable preregistration of these development choices.

The 160-run split estimates are 74.375% → 64.375% for 120444365 and 94.375% → 85.625% for 183374279: costs **10.000** and **8.750 percentage points**. They are compatible with a useful trade-off; they do not undo the first failed 40-run decision. The provided CSVs and log 79 already contain the failure, so the problem is public presentation and the causal claim “was noise,” not missing raw results.

## Proposed replacement wording and release conditions

Suggested replacement for the split-choice passage:

> The 40-realisation design-selection rule in decision 77 was not met: TIC 120444365 lost 15 percentage points of on-time recovery. We nevertheless chose the timing split after inspecting the design results because of its reduction of alternating-offset recoveries and the poor power of the literal per-event alternative. Expanding the same phase sequence to 160 realisations estimated a 10-point loss; this was a post-result refinement, not a new independent or prespecified acceptance test. The selected procedure is now fixed for calibration and for the S106 tests.

Suggested replacement for the edge claim:

> We selected pooled trims of 1.5 d after long-gap resumes and 0.5 d before gaps exceeding 0.1 d. The profiles show large edge excursions, but these cuts do not satisfy the literal requirement that every later 0.25-d bin have an exceedance fraction at most 0.03. They are a design choice informed by the profiles and coverage trade-offs. Full per-target profiles and calibration under the selected cuts are reported.

Suggested replacement for outcome interpretation:

> RECOVERED denotes compatibility with the specified common-box and parity-split scoring rule. It does not demonstrate that every event individually meets the original timing and duration limits, exclude a half-period eclipsing binary, or identify the companion. INCONSISTENT requires a coherent displaced common-box signal at the higher SNR threshold; its absence is not evidence that the ephemeris is correct. INSUFFICIENT and AMBIGUOUS are not confirmations.

**Minimum release conditions:** retain the completed R1 evidence, resolve R2–R8, correct the unsupported wording/provenance in R9–R12, and publish the completed hash-linked package with all seven dated amendments and a deterministic definition of the final adjudicated outcome. R13 should be hardened without altering declared scientific thresholds. The known single-timescale beta, high-SNR consistency gate, idealised injections, median baseline and asymmetric thresholds need not each independently block an explicitly limited operational procedure; they do block claims of calibrated significance or faithful physical duration testing unless the relevant evidence supports those claims. No further access to S106 photometry is needed to make these corrections.
