Correct those bounded issues and the inaccurate “thresholds unchanged” statement, regenerate the affected hashes/amendment, and re-check. There is no need to change the frozen prediction CSV or redesign the successful search logic. A corrected standalone package should be published promptly before test photometry is examined; this report does not authorize publication or data access.

## Is validator-side binding sufficient?

**Yes, conceptually.** The scorer need not embed selection/acquisition paths merely to imitate the round-4 clock interface. If the required final validation verifies a consistent selection, acquisition, config and exact LC digest, the same digest in the scorer output connects the result to those acquired bytes. The ordinary pipeline-9999 and altered-byte substitutions now fail that chain in the supplied tests (`test_317134140_lock.py:121–127`). A second selection cannot be substituted while retaining an acquisition tied to the first selection's hash.

Retain the complete final record set and its digests with the released result; a machine-readable final binding receipt would help, but is not a separate demand to alter the scorer. The current remaining problem is that the validator does not yet verify everything its selection summary claims (R1–R2).

Refusing NONE and AMBIGUOUS instead of selecting an arbitrary product is appropriate. NONE before the deadline is a pending/unavailable-now state, not automatically the final UNAVAILABLE outcome. The first-check policy and limitation are acceptable as a disclosed **first observed listing** policy with preserved monitoring/query records; a current product list alone cannot certify that an older version never existed. The saved S107 example is internally consistent: its canonical response hash matches, its acquisition names the exact selection hash, and its LC hash agrees with the saved S107 training summary. I read only those JSONs, not the downloaded file, and did not independently authenticate that live download or its history.
