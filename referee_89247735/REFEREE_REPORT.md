# Referee report

**Manuscript:** "A 1%-deep, 18.32-day transiting companion candidate to the K dwarf TIC 89247735 (SCR J1820−4533) from TESS full-frame images" (F. Molina, single author; `paper/main.tex`, compiled against AASTeX 6.3.1 for this review)
**Journal:** The Astronomical Journal
**Report date:** 2026-10-18
**Materials consulted:** the LaTeX source and macros, the frozen JSON/CSV products under `results/vet_qlp/89247735/`, the earlier external review, the literature survey, the prior-detection sweep, the QLP light curves on disk, Gaia DR3 (live query), Crossref/arXiv/Zenodo/ASCL (live), the MAST QLP and TARS pages, the MIT QLP release notes, and the concurrent public analysis by A. Zarco. My recomputations are in `check_lightcurves.py`, `check_derived.py` and `check_refs.py` in this directory (outputs: `check_lightcurves.json`, `check_refs.json`).

## (a) Summary and recommendation

The paper reports four flat-bottomed, ~1%-deep, ~4-h transit-like events of a T = 11.1 K dwarf in TESS QLP full-frame-image photometry (Sectors 66, 93 ×2, 104), on a single linear ephemeris with P = 18.323172 d, and argues from a per-pixel difference-image analysis, an independent FFI pipeline (TARS), a same-CCD field control, odd/even and secondary-eclipse tests and the Gaia DR3 radial-velocity scatter that the signal is on the target and that the companion is a 0.76 R_Jup object of mass below ~25–30 M_Jup. The object is presented as a transiting companion candidate (giant planet or brown dwarf) with a pre-registered, hash-stamped ephemeris, and no validation is claimed.

I reproduced the core measurements from the public QLP files: the four mid-times to ≤ 0.13 min (Sectors 66 and 93), the per-event depths to ≤ 60 ppm, the direct Sector-93 period (18.3220 ± 0.0019 d), the four-event odd/even difference (1.9σ), the linear ephemeris and its covariance, the stellar density, radius, equilibrium temperature, insolation and the Gaia RV mass bound (checklist in section e). The detection itself is credible, the four events are real features of the data, and an independent analyst (Zarco) obtains the same period to 1 part in 10^7 and the same T0 to within a minute. The candidate deserves publication.

The manuscript, however, is not acceptable in its present form. Several central claims are incorrect, unsupported or internally inconsistent, and the paper's own pre-registered predictions contain a systematic error that would defeat the stated purpose of the pre-registration:

1. The predicted transit times in UTC (abstract, Section 6, the deposited CSV) are BJD_TDB values converted to UTC without removing the barycentric light-travel time. The geocentric times differ by −4 to +8 min (epoch 159: 02:09 UTC, not 02:17; epoch 162: 01:28, not 01:33). For a paper whose falsifiable content is a timed prediction with a "±15 min" window, this must be fixed in the paper and in a new frozen version of the file.
2. The statement that the fourth transit "excludes a 36.65-d eclipsing binary with equal primary and secondary eclipses" is logically wrong. Epochs 79, 118, 119 and 138 are all integer multiples of P and therefore all consistent with a 2P binary whose primary and secondary eclipses alternate; the fourth event adds one more odd/even pair and nothing else. The 2P twin-binary scenario is excluded (or not) by the odd/even depth agreement, the transit shape and the pixel localisation, and the text must say so.
3. The companion radius rests on a transit-shape fit to QLP's spline-detrended flux (depth 9,900 ppm with k = 0.0996) while the paper's own Figure 1 and Table 1 show the raw-flux events at 10,750–11,370 ppm; the model is visibly shallower than the data in three of the four panels. Whether and how dilution was corrected is never stated (QLP SAP flux is crowding-corrected with the TIC contamination ratio; the TESScut apertures are not; QLP Sector 104 is a PSF product). The stellar radius error (TIC: 0.785 ± 0.066 R_sun, 8%) is omitted from the ±0.3 R_Earth quoted; the realistic uncertainty is ±0.7–0.9 R_Earth and the central value is 8.7–9.2 R_Earth depending on the depth adopted. No independent stellar radius (SED, Gaia GSP-Phot gives 0.726 R_sun) is used.
4. The exclusion of the G = 15.49 neighbour at 8–9.5″ (0.35 px) is quoted with formal pixel errors of 0.03–0.06 px and Δχ² = 83–214, far below the published TESS localisation floors (7.5″ SPOC, 15″ LEO-Vetter, ~5″ at high S/N). Empirical per-epoch centroid scatter from off-transit windows (Zarco: 0.15–0.29 px per epoch) is 3–8× the formal error, and the WCS-vs-image systematic (0.16–0.17 px) is half the target–neighbour separation. The test needs a calibrated error budget and a WCS-independent relative formulation before "excludes" can be used.
5. The Sector-104 event is described as sitting on an "orbit-start ramp"; in the file it lies 4.8 d into orbit 220, and the 140 flagged cadences fall after egress (BTJD 4197.45–4198.23) and in the reaction-wheel calibration windows. The "sole outlier" justification for the ad hoc 6-min systematic is not supported by the paper's own v2 residuals (−1.3, +1.8, +1.5, −1.8σ). The search context is also under-reported: the same search produced at least two other "three-event", 18.09-d, ~0.8%-deep signals (TIC 291195602, TIC 61138014) that the project's own triage rejects because a single event in a heavily flagged region of Sector 94 dominates the stacked signal, and the filtered inverted-null statistics quoted do not match the raw null distribution on disk (31 of 300 inverted stars exceed SDE 12 before filtering). The reader needs the filtered real/null counts and the dispositions.

Together with the bibliography (five entries with wrong DOIs/arXiv IDs or invented titles, one inline citation without a bib entry), the AAS-style problems (abstract 286 words, a wrong UAT number, a seven-column `deluxetable` that overprints the left column, a nine-panel Figure 2 that is unreadable at the published size, a "this is a preprint" sentence in the acknowledgments) and the missing items a referee expects for an FFI candidate (Gaia astrometric-quality table, independent stellar radius, limb-darkening justification, a TRICERATOPS NFPP or an explicit reason not to, reaction-wheel windows, depth consistency across extractions in one table), my recommendation is:

**Major revision.** None of the problems kills the candidate, and all are fixable with the data already in hand, but the pre-registered predictions and three of the headline quantitative statements must be corrected before the paper can be the public record it is meant to be.

## (b) Major points

### M1. The predicted UTC times omit the barycentric light-travel correction (abstract; Section 6, "Pre-registered predictions"; Section 4.5; `predicted_transits_2026_2027_v3.csv`)

The CSV's `utc` column and every UTC time in the text are `Time(BJD_TDB).utc` with no Rømer term. I recomputed the geocentric arrival times for the target (RA 275.2237°, Dec −45.5612°) with astropy (`check_lightcurves.py` companion snippet in the transcript; see table):

| epoch | BJD_TDB (paper) | paper UTC | geocentric UTC | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 146 | 2461343.39521 | 2026-10-29 21:27 | 21:31 | +4.0 min |
| 152 | 2461453.33425 | 2027-02-16 20:00 | 20:04 | +4.5 min |
| 156 | 2461526.62694 | 2027-05-01 03:01 | 02:57 | −4.6 min |
| 159 | 2461581.59645 | 2027-06-25 02:17 | 02:09 | −7.9 min |
| 162 | 2461636.56597 | 2027-08-19 01:33 | 01:28 | −4.9 min |

Zarco's table (which states "barycentric light-travel time toward the target removed") gives 02:08 and 01:27 for the same two epochs, consistent with my values to within the 1-min ephemeris difference. An observer who plans on the paper's "02:17 UTC" would be 8 min late at the June 2027 transit and would use up half of the ±15 min window the paper quotes. Fix: give BJD_TDB as the primary quantity (it is), state explicitly that the UTC column is geocentric with the light-travel time removed, recompute it, and freeze a v4 of the CSV (keeping v1–v3 unchanged as the paper already does). The abstract's "2027 June 25 and 2027 August 19" survive; the clock times do not.

### M2. The 36.65-d twin-eclipsing-binary scenario is not closed by the fourth transit (Section 4.1, second paragraph; Summary)

"In particular, the four events exclude a 36.65-d eclipsing binary with equal primary and secondary eclipses, which the three-event data had not." For a circular binary with P' = 2P, eclipses occur every P, alternately primary and secondary; epochs 79, 118, 119 and 138 are all consistent with that. The three-event data (79, 119 same parity; 138 opposite) already had one of each kind; the fourth adds a second "even" eclipse. What the data say about P' = 2P is therefore (i) the odd/even depth ratio, 11,319 ± 216 vs 10,746 ± 212 ppm (1.9σ, ratio 1.053 ± 0.029), which does not exclude equal eclipses; (ii) the flat-bottomed shape with T23/T14 ≈ 0.8, which excludes a grazing twin geometry on the target but not a diluted partial eclipse on a faint neighbour; and (iii) the pixel localisation, which is the only argument against the neighbour. The sentence should be replaced by exactly that reasoning, and the Summary's "every period longer than 18.32 d is excluded by data" (also in the project report) should be dropped. The same paragraph's coprime argument is correct for all other P' > P (differences 39, 1, 19 cannot all be multiples of 1.5, 2, 2.5, …).

### M3. Depth, dilution and the companion radius (Sections 3.3, 5, Table 2, Figure 1, Table 1 note)

(a) The shape fit (k = 0.0996 ± 0.0020, model depth 9,924 ppm plus limb darkening) was made to the "transit-masked, biweight-detrended QLP flux", i.e. to `DET_FLUX`, which QLP has already spline-detrended without a transit mask and which the paper itself says "biases the search depth low". Table 1 and Figure 1 are built from the raw `SAP_FLUX` with local baselines, where the events are 10,750–11,370 ppm. Fitting the paper's own MCMC shape with a free depth scale to the raw-flux events (my `check_lightcurves.py`) gives scale factors 1.007, 1.000, 1.076 and 0.975 for epochs 79, 118, 119, 138: the Sector-66 and epoch-119 panels of Figure 1 show the black curve above the data in the transit bottom. The paper's quoted "5–10% depth systematic ... adds ≈ 0.3 R_Earth" is an admission that the headline radius comes from the wrong data product. Fit the shape to the same raw-flux, local-baseline data the figure shows (or to TESScut aperture photometry with an explicit dilution model), and quote that radius.

(b) Dilution is never discussed, yet it sets the depth. The TIC contamination ratio is 0.27 (target fraction 0.787). QLP light curves are de-blended using the TIC (Guerrero et al. 2021 describe the procedure; the MAST product description only calls `SAP_FLUX` "normalized"), which would explain why the QLP events sit at ~11,000 ppm while 1–3-px TESScut apertures give 8,000–4,300 ppm and Zarco's Gaia-flux dilution model gives 11,300–12,200 ppm after correction; the ratio of the two (0.73–0.79) matches the TIC target fraction. Sector 104 is a TGLC-method PSF product, which de-blends differently again. The paper must state which fluxes are dilution-corrected and how, and give one table of per-event depths in (i) QLP SAP, (ii) QLP DET, (iii) TESScut at two apertures with the dilution factor, (iv) TARS. The numbers exist (Sections 4.2 and 4.3 quote several) but are scattered through the prose.

(c) R_p = 8.5 ± 0.3 R_Earth uses R_⋆ = 0.785 R_sun with no error. TIC v8.2 gives 0.785 ± 0.066 (8.4%); propagating it gives ±0.74 R_Earth (`check_derived.py`). Gaia GSP-Phot gives 0.726 R_sun; Zarco's SED fit gives 0.74 ± 0.04 R_sun and 8.7 ± 0.6 R_Earth with free dilution. Depending on the depth adopted the central value is 8.7 (10,235 ppm), 9.0 (11,000 ppm) or 9.2 R_Earth (11,560 ppm) with the TIC radius. The paper should (i) adopt a stellar radius with an error, preferably from an SED or Gaia luminosity plus T_eff, with the TIC value as a cross-check; (ii) quote R_p with the full error; and (iii) note that at 9 R_Earth the object is further above the 8 R_Earth validation cut (Section 5), which strengthens, not weakens, the paper's own framing.

(d) Durations are inconsistent. Table 2 lists "Duration (h) 4.0", the abstract says "≈ 4.0 h long", and Section 3.3 says the "expected central-transit duration ... 4.4 h, matches the observed 4.0 h". But the shape parameters in the same table (k = 0.0996, a/R_⋆ = 30.9, b = 0.44) imply T14 = 4.57 h and T23 = 3.56 h; the 4.0 h is the BLS box width (72 cadences × 200 s), which for a limb-darkened trapezoid lies between T23 and T14. Zarco's fits give T14 = 4.50–4.60 h. Replace "Duration 4.0 h" by T14 and T23 from the fit, and compare the 4.6-h T14 with the expected value (4.7 h for b = 0 with a/R_⋆ from Kepler's law and the TIC density; 4.25 h if the (1 + k) factor is dropped, which is presumably where 4.4 h came from).

(e) The limb-darkening coefficients are not given. The code uses (u1, u2) = (0.40, 0.25) for every star; for a 4400 K dwarf in the TESS band the tabulated quadratic coefficients are of order (0.5, 0.2) (e.g. Claret 2017; Zarco adopts 0.52, 0.18). State the coefficients and their source, or fit them with a prior.

### M4. The pixel-level exclusion of the G = 15.49 neighbour is overstated (Section 4.2, Figure 2)

The claim "They exclude the G = 15.49 neighbour" rests on (i) a flux-weighted centroid of positive-depth pixels within 3.5 px, with 0.03–0.04 px bootstrap errors, and (ii) a free-position PSF fit with Δχ² = 214, 194, 83 (neighbour minus target) from white-noise per-pixel errors. Three problems:

- The quoted statistical errors are formal. Zarco measured the scatter of the same difference-image centroid over 59–71 off-transit windows and obtains 0.14–0.29 px per epoch, 3–8× larger. At that level the neighbour's 0.35 px offset is excluded at ~2σ per epoch, ~4σ for four epochs, not at the 9–15σ the Δχ² values imply. The paper must calibrate its errors on fake-transit windows (the standard since Bryson et al. 2013 and Twicken et al. 2018) and report the result.
- The reference position is a WCS-projected catalogue position, and the paper itself reports a 0.16–0.17 px WCS-vs-image systematic (half the target–neighbour separation) and a 0.13 px proper-motion shift. The offsets quoted change by up to 0.13 px between the J2000 and proper-motion-corrected references (0.18/0.04/0.16 vs 0.17/0.10/0.03 px). A test that depends on the absolute WCS cannot exclude a source 0.35 px away at the claimed confidence. Formulate the test relatively: fit the star position and the loss position in the same image frame, and compare the (loss − star) vector with the catalogue (neighbour − target) vector (0.35 px at PA 238°). Report both components, not just the modulus.
- The paper should state the published floors (7.5″ 3σ for SPOC DV, Guerrero et al. 2021; 15″ for LEO-Vetter, Kunimoto et al. 2025; ~5″ at high S/N for PRF fits, Bryson & Kunimoto 2025) and explain why this case, at 8–9.5″, can do better: the S/N is very high (40σ per event) and the test is a two-hypothesis comparison at known positions rather than a blind offset. That argument is legitimate but has to be made, with the calibrated errors.
- "The fitted width of the loss map (0.73–0.78 px) is slightly broader than the stellar PSF (0.53–0.61 px), as expected from the 10-min cadence jitter, not from a second source." The cadence is 200 s in these sectors, and pointing jitter broadens the out-of-transit image and the loss map equally; it does not explain a loss map broader than the stellar image. Either explain the broadening (per-pixel baseline fitting, the non-linearity of the positive-only weighting, noise) or remove the sentence.
- Zarco's independent difference images give weighted-mean offsets of +1.0 ± 1.6″ E, +2.0 ± 2.0″ N and prefer the target over the 9.5″ star by Δχ² = 764 summed; the two analyses agree that the loss is on the target, and the paper would be stronger quoting that agreement than quoting formal Δχ² alone.
- The neighbour's required eclipse depth is given as 23%. With ΔG = 3.64 (flux ratio 0.035), a target flux fraction of 0.787 and a 1.02–1.10% depth it is 37–40% (`check_derived.py`); the 23% comes from dividing the 8,239 ppm detection-stage depth by 3.6%. Zarco gets 28–41% depending on the PSF model. Correct the number; it helps the argument.

### M5. Sector 104: the "orbit-start ramp" description is wrong, and the 6-min systematic is ad hoc (Sections 3.2, 3.1, Table 1 note)

In the QLP file the Sector-104 event (BTJD 4196.81) lies 4.82 d after the start of orbit 220 (orbit 219: 4178.58–4191.77; orbit 220: 4191.99–4205.20). The 140 flagged cadences within ±10 h are not an orbit-start ramp: 28 bit-31 cadences fall inside ±2 h and the rest in short runs at 4197.45–4198.23, i.e. after egress, with further runs at 4198.61–4198.72, 4200.81–4200.91 and 4203.00–4203.11 that coincide exactly with the reaction-wheel calibration windows listed in DRN 142. The momentum dump at 4198.20 is 33 h away, as the paper says. Rewrite the description (the flagged cadences are mid-orbit scattered-light/attitude flags, and the transit precedes the first calibration window by 1.8 d), and add the reaction-wheel windows to the text since they are the obvious question for any S104 timing.

The timing systematic: the paper argues that with the QLP S104 time the four-point fit had χ² = 10.5 for 2 d.o.f. and "Sector 104 was the sole outlier". The v2 residuals are −2.9, +3.7, +2.3, −2.6 min with errors 2.2, 2.0, 1.6, 1.4 min, i.e. −1.3, +1.8, +1.5, −1.8σ: no single point is the outlier; the per-event errors are uniformly ~2× too small, as the project's own report and the external review concluded. Inflating only S104 to 6 min (and setting the global inflation to 1) reduces χ² to 0.46 and the period error to 4.2 × 10⁻⁵ d, which is smaller than the v2 value (7.0 × 10⁻⁵) obtained before the "systematic" was added and smaller than the external review's four-event QLP fit. The S104 "QLP vs TESScut" spread (4196.8043 vs 4196.8077–4196.8132) is real, but S66 and S93 also differ between products by 1–2 min (Zarco's TESScut times are 1.1–1.8 min earlier than the paper's for epochs 79, 118, 119). I recommend: (i) time all four events in the same extraction with the same baseline model (TESScut aperture photometry would be the natural choice, since it is independent of QLP's pipeline changes at S94 and of the S99–S104 timestamp issue); (ii) inflate all errors by sqrt(χ²/dof); (iii) keep the ±15 min observing window, which is appropriate either way. The paper should also report the actual number from the barycentric-timestamp check ("≤ 1 min in Sector 104") and how it was done, since the MAST notice still lists S104 as not redelivered.

### M6. The search statistics and the multiple-testing context are under-reported (Section 2.2, Section 3.1)

The paper quotes an inverted-null calibration "run through the same filters: median SDE 7.1, 99th percentile 10.6, maximum 11.6, none above 12". The raw inverted-null distribution on disk (`results/null_qlp/`, 300 stars) has median 7.6, 99th percentile 32.9, maximum 57.5 and 31 stars above SDE 12; the quoted numbers are post-filter. On the real side the search produced 763 of 3,411 stars with top-peak SDE > 12 (the candidate ranks 183rd by raw SDE), 165 survivors of the physical filters, and 39 of those with SDE > 12 (the candidate ranks third among them). Two of the survivors are three-event, 18.09-d, 7,600–8,100 ppm signals (TIC 291195602, TIC 61138014) that look like the candidate in period, depth and event count. Their triage records (`results/triage_qlp/`) show that in each case one event, at BTJD 3874.78–3874.79 (18.5 d into Sector 94, in a window where 61–62 of 72 cadences carry QLP bit 31), supplies the whole stacked signal (dominance 0.98–1.00) while the two Sector-104 "events" are at S/N between −1 and +1; the project's triage rejects them on that basis. None of this is in the paper. A referee needs: the number of real and inverted candidates after each filter, how the 38 other SDE > 12 survivors were dispositioned (and that two of them are look-alikes in period and depth), and a sentence on why the candidate differs (four individually significant events, three of them mid-orbit, equal depths, flat bottoms, on-target pixels). The inverted null's known blindness to one-signed ramps (the paper cites Thompson et al. 2018 correctly) makes this disclosure necessary rather than optional.

### M7. The secondary-eclipse limit uses a formal error that the data do not support (Section 4.1, Section 5)

I reproduce 377 ± 143 ppm at phase 0.5 in `DET_FLUX` (paper: 384 ± 160) and 381 ± 142 ppm in raw SAP with the same local-baseline estimator; all three phase-0.5 windows (S66, S93, S104) are individually positive (340–445 ppm). But the same estimator applied at 300 random phases has an empirical scatter of 298 ppm (95th percentile 412 ppm; 5.7% of random phases exceed 377 ppm), and the value doubles to ~760 ppm with a quadratic instead of linear baseline. The 2.4σ "hint" is therefore ~1.3σ empirically, and the 2σ upper limit is ~1,000 ppm, not 700, which moves the thermal-companion limit from T_eff < 2680 K to ≈ 2850 K (`check_derived.py`). Zarco's TESScut value is 4 ± 98 ppm (formal). Quote the empirical scatter, the per-sector values, and the baseline dependence; the conclusion ("no secondary; excludes all but the latest M dwarfs") survives but with honest numbers.

### M8. QLP quality bit 31: the factual statements are wrong (Sections 3.1 and 7)

"QLP quality bit 31 ... set since Sector 77 and documented on the MIT QLP page but not in the MAST product description" and "marks 20–30% of cadences per sector since Sector 77". In the files: Sector 66 (2023) already has 2,322 cadences (19%) with bit 31; Sector 93 has 18%, Sector 104 25%. The MIT release notes say the QLP flag moved from bit 13 (2^12) to bit 31 (2^30) with the EM2 products (Sector 56 onward; note of 2023-11-21) and that from Sector 77 the spacecraft bits are derived from TICA rather than SPOC headers; and the MAST data-product description does document the flag, as bit 13 "Low precision points. The cadence was excluded because low precision data occurred in a large fraction of the stars". That definition matters for the paper's argument: the flag is a field-wide, time-based quality cut, not a statement about this star, which is exactly why a transit inside it can be real. Correct both passages and cite the product description.

### M9. The companion-nature section needs tightening (Section 5)

- The Gaia RV bound. The paper's inversion of the DR3 error model is right (radial_velocity_error = sqrt(π/2 · σ²/N + 0.11²) → σ = 1.59 km/s per transit for N = 14). The K bound then depends on what "no excess scatter" means: with rv_chisq_pvalue = 0.52 (χ² ≈ 12.1 for 13 d.o.f.), no excess at p = 0.05 gives K < 2.1 km/s (21 M_Jup), at p = 0.01 K < 2.6 km/s (26 M_Jup); rv_amplitude_robust = 5.68 km/s read as 2K gives 29 M_Jup (Zarco). The text's "K ≲ 2.2–2.8 km/s and hence M_2 ≲ 25–30 M_Jup" is internally off (2.2 km/s is 22 M_Jup) and the method should be stated in one sentence with the p-value used. Cite the Gaia DR3 documentation for the formula alongside Katz et al. (2023). Note sin i ≈ 1 for a transiting companion so the bound is on M_2, not M_2 sin i.
- The "roughly even prior" compares a Kepler occurrence for 8–11.3 R_Earth planets at 12.5–25 d (0.06%) with a brown-dwarf occurrence at P < 10 d (0.2%, Csizmadia et al. 2015). The period ranges differ and the planet bin excludes 11–16 R_Earth objects that would also be 1% transits on this star only if R_⋆ were smaller; say that the comparison is indicative and quote the ranges. A referee would also like one sentence on the brown-dwarf radius–age degeneracy with a citation to a modern compilation (Carmichael 2023; Vowell et al. 2025), which the project's literature survey already collected.
- TRICERATOPS. The decision not to run it is defensible for validation, but its nearby-false-positive probability (NFPP) is a blend diagnostic that does not depend on the 8 R_Earth calibration cut. Zarco reports FPP ≲ 3 × 10⁻³ (seed-dependent, 4 × 10⁻¹⁵ to 2.7 × 10⁻³) and NFPP ~ 10⁻⁶², with all FPP in the "planet orbiting an unresolved companion" scenario. Either run it and report NFPP only, with the caveat, or state explicitly that the concurrent analysis did and what it found.
- Add a short Gaia astrometric-quality table (RUWE 1.099, astrometric_excess_noise 0.086 mas at 8.7σ, ipd_frac_multi_peak 0, ipd_gof_harmonic_amplitude 0.012, duplicated_source false, non_single_star 0; all confirmed by my query) and state the blind zone (< 0.2″ unresolved, 0.2–2″ partially). The text currently gives only RUWE.
- The hierarchical-triple / < 1″ companion channel is acknowledged but should be quantified: for a flat-bottomed 1% event with T23/T14 ≈ 0.8, what companion flux ratios still allow a stellar eclipse? Zarco's b-profile (Δχ² ≥ 40 for b ≥ 0.8, maximum dilution factor ~1.8) is the kind of statement needed.

### M10. The pre-registration narrative should be stated precisely (abstract, Sections 3.1, 4.4, 6)

The fourth transit is called an "out-of-sample confirmation" found "after a three-transit ephemeris had been frozen". It was found about one hour later, by the same project's internal review, in data that had been on disk and in the pipeline's own `ffi_pixel_S93.json` (n_events = 2) all along. It is a genuine test in the narrow sense that the event was not used in the fit, and the +3.4 min agreement is a real result, but the abstract's wording invites the reader to imagine new data. Say in one sentence that the event was recovered from already-downloaded cadences that the quality mask had removed, within hours of the freeze. Similarly, "Version 1 ... is the test of record for epochs ≥ 146" should be paired with the statement that v1's clock times carry the UTC error of M1.

## (c) Minor points, presentation and typos

1. **Abstract length.** ~286 words; the AAS limit is 250. The pixel-test Δχ² values, the TARS sentence and the control-star sentence can go.
2. **Keywords.** UAT 2119 is "Atmospheric variability", not "Exoplanet candidates" (which is not a UAT concept). Use e.g. Exoplanet detection methods (489), Transit photometry (1709), Transits (1711), Brown dwarfs (185), Exoplanets (498).
3. **Table 1** is a seven-column `deluxetable` in a two-column layout; in the compiled PDF its note runs under the left-column text (page 3). Use `deluxetable*`. The tablecomments also repeat Section 3.2 at length.
4. **Figure 2** places three full-width diagnostic PNGs (each with three panels: image, significance map, depth-vs-aperture) side by side at 0.33 textwidth: nine panels, unreadable, with the depth-vs-aperture panels duplicated in text. Remake it as a 1 × 3 (or 2 × 3) figure of the significance maps alone, with the target, the proper-motion-corrected target, the G = 15.49 neighbour (which is not individually labelled now, contrary to the caption) and the loss centroid/PSF position marked, and with a colour-bar label.
5. **Figure 1.** State in the caption that the black curve is the DET_FLUX shape and therefore shallower than the raw events (or, better, refit; see M3). Panel titles give v2 depths; say so. The legend "QLP SAP (QUALITY = 0)" is in the second panel only.

## Comparison with the concurrent analysis (Zarco, 2026-10-18)

The two analyses agree on everything that matters: the four events, the period (18.323173 vs 18.323172 d), T0 (1.2 min apart), the on-target localisation, the absence of a secondary, odd/even consistency, and the Gaia RV mass bound (29 vs 25–30 M_Jup). They differ where the manuscript is weakest: Zarco corrects for dilution explicitly, uses an SED radius with an error, fits all four events jointly in one extraction with per-epoch quadratic baselines, calibrates centroid errors on off-transit windows, runs TRICERATOPS, gives T14 = 4.5–4.6 h, and converts predictions to geocentric UTC. His radius (8.7 ± 0.6 R_Earth) and the manuscript's (8.5 ± 0.3) are consistent only because the manuscript's error is understated. Nothing in his record contradicts the detection; the manuscript should cite it as corroboration and bring its own treatment of depth, radius, errors and predictions up to the same standard.
