# Sector 106: what the data release notes say, and what it means for the seven S106 tests (2026-11-10)

**Status:** descriptive note, written before any QLP Sector 106 light curve exists (the QLP `s0106` directory still returned 404 on
2026-11-08). No Sector 106 photometry of any star was downloaded, opened or analysed. Nothing here changes a locked rule, a config or
the evaluator. The outcome of each test is whatever the locked evaluator (conformance v2.1) returns on the QLP v01 file.

## Source

TESS Data Release Notes 144 (Sector 106), read as text only on 2026-11-10:
`https://archive.stsci.edu/missions/tess/doc/tess_drn/tess_sector_106_drn144_v01.pdf`
(1,680,677 bytes, SHA-256 `3fc6c8f03058ae5426b7291c26a27b91e754c1e9d941c75daeebc1e18c682889`, dated September 03, 2026). It is a
sector-wide document, not photometry.

## What it reports

- **No timing problem.** The note reports no clock, time-stamp or barycentric-correction issue for Sector 106.
- **An unplanned data gap.** The downlink between orbits 223 and 224 was lost. The onboard recorder filled during orbit 224a and
  data collection paused from 2026-07-30 19:21:09 UTC (TJD 4252.30716, "SSR overflow") until the start of orbit 224b at
  2026-08-02 09:51:09 UTC (TJD 4254.91132). The note gives 2.39 days of science data lost and 25.43 days collected. The mission's
  orbit table does not show this gap.
- **Pointing excursions.** Sector 106 is the first full sector on a new attitude control system (ACS 5.11). Correlated pointing
  excursions leave systematic features in simple-aperture light curves, mostly near the end of the first, second and fourth
  half-orbits. SPOC put its Manual Exclude flag (QUALITY bit 8) on 3.39 days of data. That is SPOC's flagging. How QLP flags these
  cadences is unknown until QLP Sector 106 exists.

Segment times from the note's Table 1 (TJD = JD - 2,457,000): 4233.42522 to 4240.04466; 4240.25439 to 4247.86410; 4248.07383 to
4252.30716; 4254.91132 to 4261.87521.

## Forecast of eligible events

`conformance_s106_forecast_drn144.py` repeats the published forecast (`conformance_v2.1/scripts/conformance_s106_forecast.py`) with
the reported gap added. It uses the same seven gap templates (QLP gap patterns of in-sample sectors mapped onto Sector 106's orbit
breaks), the evaluator's own `edge_trim` and `coverage` functions, and the frozen predicted times. Three cases:

- **a, published:** each template as published. All 49 rows reproduce the published forecast (asserted in the script).
- **b, with the gap:** each template with the reported window (TJD 4252.30716 to 4254.91132) removed.
- **c, best case:** the note's segment times alone, with no other gap.

An event is eligible, as in the evaluator, when at least 90 % of its reference duration is covered after the edge rule and at least
half a duration of baseline is covered on each side. A test with fewer than two eligible events is INSUFFICIENT.

Reading:

- **TIC 253931796** is expected to be INSUFFICIENT. That was already so in the published forecast; the gap does not change it.
- **TIC 183374279** is INSUFFICIENT unless both of its strict epochs (245 and 246) are eligible. Epoch 246 is not eligible in five of
  the seven templates. Unchanged by the gap.
- **TIC 120444365** loses the event at TJD 4255.007 (epoch 563), which falls in the 1.5 days dropped after the resume. It was eligible
  in four of the seven templates and is eligible in none now. One template now gives fewer than two events; none did before.
- **The three many-event tests** lose at most four events in any template and keep at least five in every case.

Per-event and joint signal-to-noise values in the CSV are carried over from the published forecast (design-sector noise).

## Limits

- QLP's own gaps and flags decide eligibility, not this forecast.
- QLP's handling of the pointing excursions is not modelled, nor is any extra noise from them. TIC 183374279's strict epoch 245
  (TJD 4238.39) lies 1.65 days before the end of the first half-orbit, in the part where the note says excursions cluster.
- The note's times are spacecraft times. The barycentric correction (at most about 8 minutes) is ignored, as in the published
  forecast.

## Superseded quick check

`superseded/s106_gap_check.py` was a first quick check (DECISIONS 208). It counted an event as eligible when its duration fitted
inside the kept intervals, without the evaluator's baseline requirement or QLP's gap patterns. Its counts were too optimistic for
TIC 253931796 and TIC 183374279. It is corrected by DECISIONS 209 and by this note, and kept only for the record.
